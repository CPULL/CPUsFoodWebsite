#Weißwurst (literally 'white sausage') is a traditional Bavarian sausage made from minced veal and pork and flavored with mace and other spices. The mixture is stuffed into pork or lamb casings.
#veal, pork, tallow, lard, salt, white pepper, mace, mustard seeds, milk powder, parsley, ice, water, flour, butter, brown sugar, salt, milk, yeast, egg
#24 units
- Get [800g_ of _veal] and [800g_ of _pork], [150g_ of beef _tallow], and [200g_ of _pork fat]
- Wash and keep the meat in <water> for a couple of hours to remove any blood
- Cut into pieces, then place in the freezer for about 1 hour to ease the grinding
- Grind all meat at least twice; try to keep it as cold as possible. Once ground, place in the freezer for 10 minutes
- Prepare your spices by mixing [30g_ of _salt], [6g_ of _white pepper], [4g_ of _mace], [4g_ of ground _mustard seeds], and [50g_ of _non-fat milk powder]
- Chop also [30g_ of _parsley]. Pro-Tip: Freeze fresh <parsley> and chop it frozen; keep it cold until use
- The original recipe also adds <lemon zest>. I do not like them here
- In a food processor (I used a stand mixer), add all the meat and about {100g} of <ice> slightly crushed with cold water
- I used [150g_ _ice] and [150ml_ very cold _water] in total
- Mix for 5 minutes, keeping sure it remains cold.
- Add all the spices (except the <parsley>) and another third of <water> and <ice>, and mix another 5 minutes
- Add the <parsley> and the remaining <water> and <ice>, and mix until it becomes smooth
- In case it will not be cold anymore, place it in the freezer for 10 minutes and then continue
- Fill hog or lamb casing with the meat mixture, perforate to remove all air
- Form the links; for 15 cm length, you should get about {24} links
- Get a very big pot and fill it with <water>; add a small amount of salt in it ({1 teaspoon} for {10 liters} is more than enough)
- Bring the <water> temperature to {75°C} and put the links inside. Cook for half an hour (if your links' diameter is {30mm} 30 minutes is fine; if they are bigger, cook 5 minutes more)
- Check the <water> temperature with a thermometer; be sure it is always around {75°C}.
- A bit of fat will leak in the water; no need to remove it
- Once cooked, place it in cold <water> and clean all the scum that may have formed
- FRESH: You can keep the links in the fridge for a week
- GLASS: Place the links in a glass container, fill completely with hot salted water, seal, and place them in a pot with boiling water. Boil for 10 minutes, then let them cool. They should last 6 months
- FREEZE: Freeze your links; they should stay good for a year
- To use them, place them in boiling water for 5 minutes, and then use them as you wish, for example, for hot dogs
*Bonus: Hotdog buns
- This will produce {3} buns
- In a pan mix [40g_ of _bread flour], [24g_ of _butter], [10g_ of _brown sugar], [5g_ of _salt], and [200ml_ of _milk]
- Measure the weight
- Cook at medium heat, stirring, until it becomes dense
- Add more <milk> until you reach the previous weight
- Get [8g_ of _fresh yeast] ({5g} if dry) and mix it in the preparation; let it stay 5 mins (10 if it was dry yeast)
- Add [200g_ of _bread flour] and mix for 5 minutes; add another tablespoon of <flour> if too sticky
- Let it rise 50 minutes
- Fold the dough, and let it rise another 50 mins
- Split into {3} parts, and form {3} balls. Fold each ball on itself a few times. Let them rest 15 mins
- Fold the balls on themselves, rolling to create the buns
- Place them in a baking tray and brush them with melted <butter> (especially the borders)
- Let them rise for 1 hour / 1 hour and a half (check after 1 hour if they are fully puffed)
- Add a splash of <milk> on [1_ _egg yolk] and brush the buns on the top
- Wait 5 mins and brush them again with the yolk
- Bake in a {170°C} oven for 25 minutes; let them cool down at least 15 mins before splitting, cutting, and filling
